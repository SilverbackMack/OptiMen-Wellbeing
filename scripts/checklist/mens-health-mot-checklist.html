The Men's Health MOT Checklist

The checks worth having at every age, what's free on the NHS, and how to get each one. Tick them off as you go.

ANY AGE Start here

  • Check your testicles once a month. In or after a warm shower, roll each one between thumb and fingers. See a GP about any lump, swelling, change in shape or ache.
  • Know your blood pressure. High blood pressure usually has no symptoms. A reading of 140/90 or more at a clinic, or 135/85 or more at home, counts as high.
  • Ask about your family history. Find out whether close relatives have had heart disease, stroke, diabetes, or prostate, breast, ovarian or bowel cancer, and at what age.
  • Be honest about how you're coping. If you've had "nothing left to give" for weeks, talk to someone. In England, adults can refer themselves to NHS Talking Therapies without seeing a GP.FREE
  • Don't sit on erection problems. Ongoing problems can be an early sign of heart or blood vessel disease. GPs deal with this every day.

FROM 40 Heart and circulation

  • NHS Health Check, every 5 years.FREE · ENGLAND · AGES 40 TO 74 Blood pressure, cholesterol, weight and your 10-year risk of heart and circulation problems. Not been invited? Contact your GP practice.
  • Pharmacy blood pressure check.FREE · ENGLAND Offered at many pharmacies if you're 40 or over and haven't had it checked in the last 5 years. No appointment with a GP needed.
  • Sleep check-in. See a GP if poor sleep has lasted for months, or if you snore loudly and someone has noticed pauses in your breathing.

FROM 45 TO 50 Prostate

  • From 45, if you're at higher risk: talk to your GP about a PSA test. Higher risk means you're Black, or your father or brother has had prostate cancer.
  • From 50: you can ask your GP about a PSA test.FREE IF AGREED WITH YOUR GP There's no routine screening, so you have to ask. Your GP will go through the pros and cons with you first.
  • Getting up at night to pee, or a weak flow? It's usually a non-cancerous enlarged prostate, but only a check-up can tell. Book a GP appointment.

FROM 50 Screening that comes to you

  • Bowel cancer screening kit, every 2 years.FREE · AGES 50 TO 74 A home test kit arrives by post. Do it when it arrives. From 75 you can ask for one on 0800 707 6060 (England).
  • AAA screening in the year you turn 65.FREE · MEN · ENGLAND A 10 to 15 minute ultrasound scan of the main artery in your tummy. You'll get a letter with an appointment.
Health Checks are run differently in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Ask your GP practice or pharmacist what's offered where you live.

Before any appointment

  • Write down your main concern in one sentence. Say it first. Appointments are short.
  • List your medicines and supplements. Include creatine, which changes a kidney blood test, and finasteride, which lowers PSA.
  • Before a PSA blood test: no ejaculation or vigorous exercise, including cycling, for 48 hours.
  • Ask three questions: What are my numbers? What do they mean for me? What happens next, and when?

Five habits that move the numbers

  • Move most days. Aim for 150 minutes a week of activity that raises your breathing, plus strength work on 2 days.
  • Keep a regular bedtime. Most adults need 7 to 9 hours.
  • Drink less. No more than 14 units a week, spread over 3 or more days.
  • Don't smoke. Free local stop smoking services roughly triple your chances of quitting.
  • Eat fish twice a week, one of them oily, and at least 5 portions of fruit and vegetables a day.

Don't wait for a check-up

  • Call 999 for sudden chest pain or pressure that doesn't go away, or signs of a stroke: a drooping face, arm weakness or slurred speech.
  • Go to A&E or call 999 for sudden, severe pain in a testicle.
  • See a GP promptly about blood in your pee, poo or semen, a lump anywhere, or weight loss you can't explain.
  • Struggling right now? Call Samaritans free on 116 123, any time, or NHS 111.
This checklist is general health information for men in the UK, based on NHS, Prostate Cancer UK and Cancer Research UK guidance as of November 2026. It is not medical advice and doesn't replace a conversation with your GP or pharmacist. Guidance changes, so check nhs.uk for the latest.
